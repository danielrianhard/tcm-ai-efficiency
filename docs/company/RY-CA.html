Investment Thesis

RBC's AI story is a specific, quantified, credibly-articulated PLAN — not a realized-in-the-financials benefit. The headline is a target of CAD $700M-$1bn of "net benefit" on a run-rate basis by end-2027, restated consistently by CEO Dave McKay across Q4 2025, Q1 2026 and Q2 2026 (May 28, 2026). Crucially, management defines this figure as a BLEND of revenue uplift, cost savings, and "enterprise value" — and the definition itself has migrated across calls ("enterprise value from AI" in Q4 2025 to "revenue and cost savings" by the March 2026 investor session), so the pure internal-operational-efficiency component is never isolated or quantified. Across all five quarters there is no AI-attributed P&L outcome: the efficiency-ratio and operating-leverage gains RBC did report (US region 83%→75%, all-bank efficiency ratio -1pt, 5% all-bank operating leverage in Q1 2026) are driven by revenue growth and HSBC Canada integration, not AI, and management does not claim otherwise. What is real is heavy AI infrastructure and adoption (200+ models, RBC Assist rolled to 30,000+ employees, 24M lines of AI-assisted code, 2M policy searches/month) — genuine inputs, but activity metrics, not realized efficiency in the numbers.

Key Risks & Red Flags

What would break this thesis
  • The single biggest red flag is that the $700M-$1bn target explicitly blends revenue uplift with cost savings and "enterprise value," so it fails the operational-efficiency purity test — the mandate excludes AI revenue products, and RBC never breaks out the internal-efficiency portion.
  • Attribution is entirely absent in the actuals: every realized margin/efficiency-ratio gain over the five quarters is credibly explained by revenue growth and HSBC Canada integration, not AI, and management does not claim AI causation for them.
  • The target's definition has visibly shifted call-to-call ("enterprise value" vs "revenue and cost savings" vs "net benefit"), a promotional/moving-goalpost risk, and the supporting proof points (lines of code, token usage, searches) are classic AI-washing input metrics rather than P&L outcomes.
  • It is also a 2027 run-rate ambition, fully forward and reversible, with zero of it yet visible in the financials.
  • Additional risks the deep-dive should flag: (1) The $700M-$1B target has been repeated at identical magnitude across five quarters despite management calling progress "ahead of where we thought" — no upward revision and no disclosed interim milestone, which is soft evidence that confidence in realization is not building.
  • (2) The metric definition itself migrated (enterprise value -> revenue and cost savings -> back to enterprise value in Q2), so the target is a moving, non-GAAP, non-auditable construct; the pure internal-efficiency component is never isolated, making it impossible to ever mark the claim to actuals.
  • (3) Key-person / org-immaturity risk: the AI Group under Bruce Ross was only created in Q1 2026 (Feb/Mar), so the delivery vehicle for the 2027 target is brand new and unproven.
  • (4) The impressive activity metrics (24M lines of code, 2M searches/month) are classic vanity/input metrics with no disclosed link to outcomes, headcount, or expense leverage — high risk of AI-washing if ever presented as if it were realized efficiency.
