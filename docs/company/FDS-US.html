Investment Thesis

FactSet has a genuine, mechanism-specific, AI-attributed internal-efficiency story that has escalated across three consecutive quarters. By Q3 FY2026 (May 2026), CEO Sanoke Viswanathan stated AI coding agents author ~27% of committed code on participating teams (up from ~20% in Q2 FY2026), and management explicitly attributed a ~10% reduction in the technology workforce to AI-assisted coding productivity. Data operations show concrete headcount decoupling: 10x faster third-party data ingestion "without adding headcount" (Q1 FY2026), >25% average cuts in manual curation from four AI tools (Q2 FY2026), and >50% lower operator touch time plus a 5% Fundamentals-team reduction (Q3 FY2026). Management claims it captured >50% of a targeted 100 bps of FY2026 productivity improvement in the first half. This is stronger than a plan, but it falls short of the cleanest "realized" names because reported adjusted operating margin actually FELL to ~34% (from 36.8% YoY) as AI-infrastructure spend and higher performance comp more than offset the savings — the efficiency is being reinvested, not yet visible on the bottom line.

Key Risks & Red Flags

What would break this thesis
  • Attribution ambiguity: the ~10% tech-workforce cut coincides with a new CEO (Sanoke Viswanathan, ex-JPMorgan, in seat under a year) — a classic restructuring/cost-discipline confound dressed as AI, and the prior note's attribution to "Phil Snow" was wrong (Snow retired).
  • Bottom-line invisibility: adjusted operating margin actually COMPRESSED (~34% vs 36.8% YoY), so the efficiency is real at the activity level but is being consumed by AI-infra spend and comp — it is NOT yet a demonstrable margin tailwind, unlike cleaner realized names.
  • Promotional/measurement risk: "% of code authored by AI" and "% of engineer capacity freed" are self-reported, hard-to-audit engineering vanity metrics; the 100 bps productivity target is management-defined.
  • Durability is only ~3 quarters old and reversibility is real if a hiring rebound follows.
  • Finally, FactSet's AI narrative is heavily weighted toward AI as a revenue product, which can blur the internal-efficiency signal.
  • Add three risks the deep-dive under-weights: (1) Attribution incentive — management has a self-serving reason to badge a ~10% technology-workforce layoff as "AI productivity" rather than cost discipline or macro; the AI causation is management's framing and is not independently auditable.
  • (2) Scope inflation — the 27% code-commit and 25% capacity figures are on "teams using these tools" / "participating teams," a self-selected subset, and overstate enterprise-wide impact; company-wide code authorship is unstated and lower.
  • (3) No bottom-line proof — adjusted operating margin fell ~280 bps YoY to 34%, so the efficiency remains invisible to shareholders; if reinvestment persists, the "realized" savings never convert to earnings, weakening the case for the "realized" label vs a "reinvested/in-progress" one.
