Near Misses

Companies that passed the thin screen, earned a full deep review, and were rejected. Each was scored against the same rubric as an admitted name and fell short. They are the closest thing the universe currently has to a future admission, and they are re-tested when they next report.

Why these are worth reading
Every name here had a real, quoted, AI-attributed number — which is already rarer than it sounds. They failed on what happened next: the number measured activity rather than cost, the reported financials moved the other way, a peer achieved the same result with no AI story, or management's own explanation of its margin never mentioned the tool. This page is where the standard is actually visible.

AXP — AMERICAN EXPRESS Near Miss · scored 22

Consumer Finance  ·  call 2026-07-24  ·  source transcript

CEO disclaimed his own AI number in the next sentence

“anywhere from a 30 to 40% decrease in cycle time from a coding perspective.”Stephen Squeri, Chairman & CEO
“Now that's really not a savings because what that does is allows us to do more.”Stephen Squeri, Chairman & CEO
“a lack of acceleration in hiring of more travel representatives, more customer service reps, even as the business continues to grow”Stephen Squeri, Chairman & CEO
▲ What survived
Management does attribute flat servicing hiring to AI tools — explicit, correctly ordered attribution. But it arrives with no rep count, no baseline and no cost figure. Attribution without quantification is a lead, not evidence.
The kill
The CEO removes his own headline from contention one sentence later: the coding gain is recycled into backlog throughput, not harvested as cost. The financials agree — total expenses +12.3% on revenue +10.0%, opex ratio 150bp WORSE year over year, and 62% of the pretax increase comes from a $321mm provision release. The metric definition also drifted in a single quarter, from Q1's '30% benefit... coding and testing' to Q2's '30–40% decrease in cycle time... coding'.

Materiality. Most generous construction: ~$23mm quarterly, 0.16% of the $14,482mm expense base — below the 0.5% floor.

Watch item. Q3 2026: if servicing headcount actually declines as Squeri predicts AND the opex ratio inflects below 72%, re-open with a quantified basis. Otherwise hard reject.

FAF — FIRST AMERICAN FINANCIAL Near Miss · scored 24

Property & Casualty Insurance  ·  call 2026-07-23  ·  source transcript

Richest AI disclosure of the quarter; unit costs went the wrong way

“Using our new AI tools, we reduced the time required by 97%.”Mark Seaton, CEO
“It has now processed more than 50,000 orders, delivering 92% with no additional human review.”Mark Seaton, CEO
“We have also improved automation rates from 30% in Q1 to 34% in Q2, and so far in July, we are at 39%.”Mark Seaton, CEO
▲ What survived
Endpoint's 30%→34%→39% series is genuinely consistently defined across two quarters — the single best piece of evidence in the file. The title-plant data moat is a credible mechanism.
The kill
Revenue rose 15% on a 17.3% lift in revenue per order while order counts were FLAT to DOWN (closes 2,145/day vs 2,161). Title firms staff to order count but bill on order value, so price and commercial mix compress the expense ratio with zero productivity gain. Opex per closed order actually ROSE ~10.6% and personnel per order ~8.4%. The 97% covers a one-time 1,300-form template update. Management's own success ratio MISSED at 66% against a 60% target.
● Peer check
Stewart (STC) improved employee costs from 29.5% to 27.4% of revenue — 210bp, BETTER than FAF's 201bp — with no AI attribution anywhere. Same driver at both: commercial mix.

Materiality. Every AI figure is an activity metric with no dollar or headcount attached. Exam Assist's 50,000 cumulative orders is ~36% of ONE quarter's direct closes; Endpoint's 39% applies to one converted branch, with network rollout not due until end-2027.

Watch item. FNF, the decisive comparable, reports 2026-08-05. Re-scan after it prints and after two more Endpoint datapoints on a stable definition.

GSHD — GOOSEHEAD INSURANCE Near Miss · scored 20

Insurance Brokers  ·  call 2026-07-22  ·  source transcript

Management's own margin bridge omits the AI tool

“Lily, our AI voice assistant, now handles approximately 20% of our inbound service calls from start to finish, with performance exceeding 30% during certain periods.”Mark Jones Jr., President & COO
“intelligent case routing, which has allowed us to reinvest roughly 40 full-time service team members towards more complex and value-added interactions”Mark Miller, CEO (Q1 2026)
“comp and G&A to grow in the high teens to low 20% for the year, which will likely be in excess of core revenue growth”John Martin, CFO
▲ What survived
The peer check FAILS to kill it — Brown & Brown's margin went the other way (EBITDAC 35.7% vs 36.7%), so there is no sector tailwind explaining GSHD away. Its expansion is company-specific. The mechanism is also genuinely credible: 2.1m policies in force and labour-intensive personal-lines service calls are exactly where voice AI works.
The kill
Management bridged 30% adjusted EBITDA growth to retention of 86%, new business, carrier commission dynamics and bind rates — and did not mention AI. When management lists its own margin bridge and omits the tool, the analyst does not get to add it. The CFO then guided comp and G&A to grow AHEAD of core revenue, which is the opposite of harvesting service automation. The metric definition also narrowed: Q1's 'all inbound calls' became Q2's 'inbound service calls', mechanically inflating the ratio, so 19%→20% may be flat or down like-for-like.

Materiality. The 40 redeployed FTEs are ~$4.9mm, ~1.7% of FY2025 opex — but they were REINVESTED, not removed. Realized disclosed saving: $0. Headcount leverage also predates Lily's deployment.

Watch item. Q3 2026: one disclosure — service headcount against policies in force, or cost per service interaction, on a stable definition — would put this into scoring range quickly.

CNC — CENTENE CORP Near Miss · scored 32

Managed Health Care  ·  call 2026-07-28  ·  source transcript

Real AI saving, honestly disclosed, roughly two orders of magnitude too small to reach the financials

“Even our legal department has several high ROI adjunctive use cases in production.”Sarah London, Chief Executive Officer
“including one agent that reviews invoices from outside counsel firms and now saves us a point and a half in our legal bills every month”Sarah London, Chief Executive Officer
“reflecting continued discipline and scale, as well as product mix.”Drew Asher, Chief Financial Officer
▲ What survived
The mechanism is genuine and the disclosure is unusually restrained — outside-counsel e-billing review is a mature, auditable AI application, and London volunteered a hard percentage while calling the use cases "adjunctive" and describing AI deployed "selectively, with a strong focus on return on investment rather than broad experimentation". No revenue conflation. CNC was also the only managed-care name in the peer set to improve its ratio at all (UNH +40bp, ELV +100bp, MOH +50bp) — but that gap is revenue growth (+4.5% vs Molina −4.8%), not productivity, so the peer test did not rescue it.
The kill
The saving is 1.5% of an undisclosed sub-line-item — outside counsel spend, which appears nowhere in Centene's income statement — so it is structurally unverifiable and bounds to roughly $1.5–$15mm a year, 0.01–0.12% of annualised SG&A. Management's own bridge explains the 20bp adjusted SG&A improvement (7.1% → 6.9%) twice without AI: the CFO cites "continued discipline and scale, as well as product mix", and the release cites "leveraging of expenses over higher revenues and reduced Marketplace membership, which operates at a meaningfully higher SG&A expense ratio". The unit-cost test is fatal — membership fell 7.6% (28.00mm → 25.89mm) while SG&A dollars ROSE $67mm to $3,103mm, so SG&A per member per month rose from $36.14 to $39.96, +10.6% (+20.6% ex-PDP). The ratio fell only because revenue per member rose 13.0%. The real SG&A lever is a $480mm workforce and enterprise optimisation programme that management never attributes to AI.

Materiality. $1.5–$15mm a year on the disclosed 1.5%; 0.012–0.121% of ~$12.4bn annualised SG&A; explains 0.4–4.2% of the 20bp improvement. Below the 0.5% floor by an order of magnitude.

Watch item. Q3/Q4 2026: re-open only on (a) an absolute dollar AI-attributed SG&A saving, or disclosure of the legal spend base that makes "a point and a half" computable, AND (b) SG&A per member per month falling year over year — the test is a print below the $36.14 Q2-2025 baseline. Also watch whether any quantified share of the $480mm optimisation programme is attributed to AI-enabled workflows rather than headcount, and whether AI moves out of the legal department into claims, FWA or member services. A repeat of the legal-only anecdote with no dollar base is confirmation of immateriality, not corroboration.

UPS — UNITED PARCEL-B Near Miss · scored 18

Air Freight & Logistics  ·  call 2026-07-28  ·  source transcript

Conveyor automation wearing an AI label — and unit cost went the wrong way

“We know that the cost per piece in an automated building is about 28% lower than a non-automated building.”Carol Tomé, Chairman & CEO
“By the end of the second quarter, 68.5% of the volume in our U.S. business was flowing through an automated building compared to 64% one year ago.”Carol Tomé, Chairman & CEO
“Strong base rate growth and increased productivity in our reconfigured network contributed to revenue per piece growing 130 basis points faster than the cost per piece growth rate.”Brian Dykes, Chief Financial Officer
▲ What survived
UPS has the right SHAPE of disclosure — a repeated cost-per-piece ratio, a tracked penetration series (64% → 66.5% → 68.5%), and a package-equivalent conversion — and it beat its own quantified automation target (68% of US volume by year-end 2026) two quarters early, alongside $3.5bn of 2025 savings delivered and $3bn reaffirmed for 2026. RFID is fully deployed across all US delivery facilities and package cars and the network digital twin is a real system, not vapour. If a number is ever attached to either and it enters the CFO's bridge, UPS becomes a live candidate quickly.
The kill
The 28%/68.5%/337mm-package evidence is not about artificial intelligence. It describes a UPS facility classification — conveyors, sortation and robotics, 127 automated buildings at end-2025 with 24 more planned — and Tomé's answer to Jordan Aliger (Goldman Sachs) never uses the word AI. The call's four AI references ("AI as the brain", an "AI-powered digital twin", "Our AI is constantly tracking network performance") carry no number, no named system and no baseline; joining them to the 28% is an inference the company never made. The 28% has been stated identically on three consecutive calls (Q4-2025, Q1-2026, Q2-2026), never dated and never reconciled to the segment P&L — a costing constant used to justify capex, not a measured saving. Decisively, unit cost moved the wrong way: revenue per piece +9.3% less the CFO's own 130bp spread implies cost per piece ROSE ~8.0% (adjusted arithmetic +8.5%, GAAP +17.2%) in the very quarter automation penetration rose 4.5pp. The full 100bp US Domestic adjusted margin gain reconciles arithmetically to price over cost, and Dykes' bridge names base rates, customer mix, fuel and "our reconfigured network" — automation and AI appear nowhere in it.

Materiality. Taken at face value the penetration shift is ~126bp of blended cost per piece, ~$170mm a quarter / ~$690mm annualised against a $13.7bn adjusted US Domestic cost base — material in size, but zero of it currently attributable to AI, and swamped by an 850bp actual increase in cost per piece.

Watch item. Q3 2026 (late Oct): (1) does adjusted cost per piece turn negative year over year — with the Amazon glide-down complete and ~2mm pieces/day gone, Q3 is the first clean read without the volume-deleverage alibi; (2) does the 28% move, get dated or get reconciled — a fourth identical print confirms it as a costing constant and it should be permanently discounted; (3) does automation or AI enter the CFO's margin bridge, which is the admission gate; (4) any number attached to RFID or the digital twin — misload rate, hours per building, planning cycle time. That, not the 28%, would be UPS's first genuine criteria-A datapoint.

WM — WASTE MANAGEMENT Near Miss · scored None

Environmental & Facilities Services  ·  call 2026-07-29  ·  source transcript

Reopen denied — a dollar sign newly attached to a decade-old camera platform, and unit cost rose

“our Smart Truck platform, which is a combination of artificial intelligence and other forms of technology, certainly, $300 million of run rate EBITDAs is significant.”Jim Fish, Chief Executive Officer (Q&A)
“more than $300 million of annual run rate operating EBITDA through service upgrades, optimized routing, and lower operating costs”prepared remarks, Q2-2026 call
“That technology on the truck I'm speaking of has been around for probably closer to a decade.”John Morris, President — Q1-2026 call, 2026-04-29
“we limited the increase in collection operating costs to less than 1.7% compared to the second quarter of 2025”John Morris, President
“margin expanded 40 basis points, driven by a strong price to cost spread and continued cost reductions from technology and automation”David Reed, Chief Financial Officer
▲ What survived
WM has a real, decade-matured telematics and imaging estate — every commercial and residential truck outfitted, over 300 million images a year, roughly 95% processed without a human touch — and that is a genuine AI workload at scale. If the company ever decomposes the $300m into an AI-attributable, cost-only figure with a baseline and vintage, WM becomes a live candidate quickly.
The kill
WM was cut on 2026-07-21 for failing the AI-attribution and materiality bar; a cut is not re-litigated without NEW evidence that overcomes the recorded cut reason. The $300m figure is new, but Smart Truck is not: Morris disclosed the platform on the pre-cut Q1-2026 call and dated the hardware to "closer to a decade". The figure is therefore an accumulated program total newly given a run-rate label, not a saving delivered since the cut, and it is arithmetically impossible as an incremental item — $300m is 114bp of margin on FY26 revenue guidance against 40bp of actual margin expansion, inside a 140bp gross line the CFO attributes first to price-to-cost spread while core price runs 6.3% and yield 3.9%. Annualised over the platform's life it is roughly $30m, 0.17% of the $18.2bn opex base, three times below the materiality floor. Attribution fails independently: Fish twice refuses the AI framing ("I don't look at it as just AI"), nobody ever sizes the AI share, and one of the three named benefit sources — "service upgrades" — is revenue capture, not opex. The recycling "30% improvement in labor cost per ton" says automation and never says AI; it is a capex benchmark against legacy facilities. Unit cost moved the wrong way: collection opex +1.7% on volumes -0.4% is +2.1% per unit, and the sub-60% opex ratio is compressed by price in the denominator. Technology appears in the CFO's bridge only as a -40bp HEADWIND, exactly cancelling the +40bp net expansion.

Materiality. $300m against implied FY26 opex of ~$18.2bn is 1.65% as stated, clearing the 0.5% floor on paper. But it is a stock, not a flow: gross EBITDA blending revenue and cost, no accumulation period disclosed, on a platform roughly ten years old. Annualised incrementally it is ~$30m, 0.17% of opex — below the floor by three times.

Watch item. Q3 2026 (late Oct): (1) is the $300m decomposed into AI-versus-cameras/telematics/routing and into service-upgrade revenue versus opex reduction; (2) is a baseline and vintage given so an incremental annual delta can be reconciled to the Collection & Disposal segment P&L; (3) does collection operating cost growth print BELOW volume growth, against this quarter's +1.7% cost on -0.4% volume; (4) does technology move from the headwind side of the CFO's bridge to a named, quantified benefit line. A repeat of "$300 million" unchanged with no reconciliation is confirmation of the UPS costing-constant pattern, not corroboration.

AJG — ARTHUR J GALLAGHER Near Miss · scored 25

Insurance Brokers  ·  call 2026-07-30  ·  source transcript

A CFO validating an analyst's arithmetic is not a plan

“That math would produce 600 basis points of margin expansion.”Doug Howell, Chief Financial Officer
“Maybe there's 400 basis points there in the way we look at it.”Doug Howell, Chief Financial Officer
“I think it'll take us three to five years to fully realize those levels.”Doug Howell, Chief Financial Officer
“Our productivity and quality efforts again delivered another terrific quarter of underlying margin expansion of 50 basis points.”Doug Howell, CFO — the actual margin bridge, with no AI in it
▲ What survived
The framework is specific and layered (5% production / 10-15% support / 20-30% back office) rather than sloganeering, and it was given by the CFO on the record. AJG's own forward-looking statements now itemise "AI-washing" as a risk factor, which is a mark of unusual self-awareness.
The kill
Gallagher's case rests on a single CFO answer to an analyst's hypothetical. Howell did not table a plan; he validated David Motemaden's arithmetic and then immediately haircut it by a third, using the word "maybe" four times while constructing it. A quantification management declines to carry into guidance is not a plan, and FY26 guidance was reaffirmed unchanged at 40-60bps. Zero AI dollars appear in the quarter. The margin bridge that does exist credits "productivity and quality efforts" — the pre-existing programme, not AI. The peer set closes it: in the same quarter AON expanded adjusted operating margin 70bps and attributed roughly 60bps of that to a named, dollarised, NON-AI restructuring programme ($25m in the quarter, $100m in 2026 toward $450m by 2027), while MMC executes a $400m "Thrive" programme whose stated levers include right-shoring. Gallagher's 50bps is at or below what its two largest peers delivered with no AI attribution at all, and below what one peer achieved through severance alone. A thesis that cannot distinguish itself from its peer group's severance line does not enter the database.

Materiality. 400-600bps would be enormous if realized. The realized figure this quarter is $0 — no AI benefit is dollarized or expressed in basis points anywhere on the call.

Watch item. Q3 2026 or the next Investor Day: does AJG restate the 400-600bps as a COMPANY target with a named base year and a dollar-denominated programme (as AON does with $450m-by-2027), and does the FY margin guide move above 40-60bps? Absent both, this stays out.

ICLR — ICON PLC Near Miss · scored 22

Life Sciences Tools & Services  ·  call 2026-07-29  ·  source transcript

Four deleted words inverted the quote — the 30% predates the AI

“SmartDraft will now have a Claude back end that helps us build on the progress we've already made, taking 30% out of the time of negotiating clinical trial contracts”Barry Balfe, Chief Executive Officer
“which are really more directly related to productivity than capability”Barry Balfe, CEO — the tail of the same sentence, downgrading his own claim
“the benefits of that mix effect as we talked about, as well as obviously the cost actions that we have taken”Nigel Clerkin, CFO — the margin bridge, with no AI in it
▲ What survived
SmartDraft is a genuinely named system with a genuinely quantified cycle-time effect, stated by the CEO on the record, and the Anthropic collaboration is real and multi-year. If the benefit is retained and gets a post-Claude baseline, this becomes testable quickly.
The kill
The quotation does not say what the case needs it to say. The thin screen's ellipsis deleted "that helps us build on the progress we've already made" — four words that invert the meaning. The 30% is progress already earned by the pre-Claude, rules-based SmartDraft; the Claude back end arrives prospectively. The ICON-Anthropic collaboration was announced 2026-07-28, one day before the call, entirely in future tense, and does not mention SmartDraft at all. The number carries no baseline, no scope and no dollar value, and appears in no company IR document — spoken remarks only. The beneficiary is unresolved: clinical trial site-contract negotiation is billable study start-up work performed for sponsors, so a cycle-time reduction there may be a sponsor-facing competitive claim rather than an ICON margin item, and management did not say which. The margin bridge fails cleanly — Clerkin credits pass-through mix, cost actions and resourcing, and Balfe's own release quote credits "disciplined cost management", with AI absent from every driver. $20.9M of Q2 restructuring ($31.0M YTD) for "elimination of redundant positions" independently explains the cost actions. The peer set removes the last doubt: IQVIA booked $63m of restructuring in the same quarter ($114m 1H, roughly 3x ICON's) and attributed none of it to AI, while Medpace turned 17.2% revenue growth into a 90bp SG&A-ratio improvement with no AI and no restructuring at all. Every element of ICON's financial story is being reproduced by peers without an AI narrative.

Materiality. Unquantifiable. Q2 direct costs were $1,584.2M on $2,063.5M revenue and ICON's cost base is overwhelmingly billable delivery labour; contract negotiation is one sub-process inside study start-up. Cannot be shown to clear the ~0.5% of opex floor.

Watch item. Q3/Q4 2026: does ICON quantify the Claude-enabled SmartDraft benefit in dollars or as a stated bps contribution to gross margin, AND state explicitly that the benefit is retained rather than passed to sponsors? A restated cycle-time figure with a post-Claude baseline distinct from the pre-Claude 30% is the minimum threshold.

MKL — MARKEL GROUP Near Miss · scored 18

Property & Casualty Insurance  ·  call 2026-07-29  ·  source transcript

Best AI deployment story of the run, monetized as revenue and invisible to the expense line

“the reduction in time to have an initial risk assessment in front of an underwriter has fallen by between 50%-90%”Simon Wilson, Chief Executive Officer, Markel Insurance
“The result is higher levels of better quality business, with the WI portfolio in London growing by 50% in the year since we deployed Harvey.”Simon Wilson, CEO Markel Insurance — converting the metric into revenue
“The improvement was driven by more favorable prior year reserve development and a slightly lower expense ratio”Markel CFO — the margin bridge, with no AI in it
“AI is only beneficial if it improves our service to customers while generating returns on capital over time.”Simon Wilson, CEO Markel Insurance — self-qualification
▲ What survived
This is the richest AI DEPLOYMENT disclosure in the run — named vendors (Harvey AI, Bain, Cytura), a named new unit (Cortex, launched in wholesale and specialty), a dated capital vehicle (February AI accelerator fund, nine ideas into production in a single month), and a scoped footprint of six classes rewired over $500m of existing GWP. The technology is not in doubt. The accounting visibility is.
The kill
Markel fails the threshold test before any peer comparison is needed: it does not disclose an expense ratio at all, in either the Q2 release or the Q1 or Q2 calls, so there is no financial series in which an AI-driven expense benefit could ever be observed. The CFO's "a slightly lower expense ratio" is an adjective, not a number. The combined ratio of 93% versus 97% is fully accounted for by items management itself identifies as non-operating: favourable prior-year reserve development, offset by $41m of Middle East catastrophe losses and a two-point drag from the exited Global Reinsurance division. The 50-90% cycle-time gain is real and impressive, but Wilson converts it into a revenue claim in the same breath — the London W&I portfolio growing 50% — and underwriters are redeployed, not removed. Under this methodology that is an AI-as-revenue thesis: logged, never scored. The Bain & Company partnership compounds it — Cortex's underwriting tools were designed jointly with a strategy consultancy, making consultancy-led operating-model redesign the more parsimonious explanation for any operating improvement that eventually appears. And "$500 million of existing GWP" is a PREMIUM denominator, not an expense denominator, so materiality against opex cannot be established even in principle. The peer set forecloses the remainder: in the same quarter W.R. Berkley improved its combined ratio 160bps while holding its expense ratio unchanged at 28.5% to the tenth of a point, RLI's deteriorated 110bps on cats and Cincinnati Financial's 590bps on cats. Q2 2026 P&C combined ratios moved on catastrophe load and reserve development, in both directions, with no AI content. Markel moved the same way, for the same reasons, and disclosed less about its expenses than any peer in the comparison.

Materiality. Cannot be established. No expense figure of any size is disclosed, so the ~0.5%-of-opex floor cannot be tested even in principle.

Watch item. Q3 2026 or the 2026 10-K: does Markel begin disclosing a Markel Insurance expense ratio as a standing metric, and does management attribute a quantified basis-point movement in it to Cortex/Harvey — separately from GWP growth and separately from the Bain engagement? A disclosed expense ratio is the precondition; without it MKL is permanently unscoreable on this thesis regardless of how good the technology is.
